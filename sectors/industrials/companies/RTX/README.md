# RTX — RTX Corporation

[Sector](../../README.md) · [Master index](../../../../INDEX.md)

> Evidence snapshot through 2026-10-20. Research status is recorded in metadata; this is not a live market feed.

## Business overview

Reported: RTX supplies commercial aerospace systems, aircraft engines and defense technologies. It combines aircraft production and aftermarket exposure with government procurement. [S01]


## Revenue model and segments

Reported: The three segments are Collins Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney and Raytheon. Revenue includes equipment sales, parts, repairs, engine support and defense contracts. [S01]


## Products and services

Reported: Examples include Collins aircraft systems, Pratt & Whitney GTF and F135 engines, and Raytheon Patriot, AMRAAM and radar systems. [S01]


## Customers and end markets

Reported: Customers include airframe manufacturers, aircraft operators, U.S. government agencies and international buyers. Commercial aviation and defense create distinct demand drivers. [S01]


## Industry and value chain

Reported: Original engine and aircraft-system placements create later service opportunities. Supplier reliability, overhaul capacity and regulatory approval affect delivery and service economics. [S01]


## Competitive landscape

Reported: The filing describes competition in aircraft equipment, spares and defense systems. Defense suppliers may also be partners or competitors on different programs. [S01]


## Moat and differentiation

Interpretation: installed engines and certified systems can support aftermarket durability. Alternative parts suppliers, pricing competition and maintenance obligations limit the advantage. [S01]


## Growth drivers

Reported: Backlog increased from USD 289 billion at June 30, 2026 to USD 340 billion at September 30, 2026. Commercial backlog moved from USD 170 billion to USD 176 billion and defense from USD 119 billion to USD 164 billion. Third-quarter awards included multiyear Tomahawk and AMRAAM production contracts whose terms were not yet fully definitized. [S06] [S05]

Reported: In the third quarter of 2026, Collins commercial aftermarket sales grew 9% year over year, Pratt & Whitney commercial aftermarket sales grew 21%, and Raytheon total sales grew 14%. [S05]

Management guidance: The July results reported commercial aftermarket and defense growth. Management plans capacity and productivity improvements; future backlog conversion still depends on execution. [S03] [S06]


## Financial performance

Reported: For July 1–September 30, 2026, GAAP sales were USD 24,965 million versus USD 22,478 million in the comparable 2025 quarter. Net income attributable to common shareowners was USD 2,583 million and diluted EPS was USD 1.89. [S05]

Reported: For the quarter ended September 30, 2026, company-defined non-GAAP adjusted diluted EPS was USD 2.26 versus USD 1.70 a year earlier. The adjusted figure includes USD 0.25 of IEEPA cost recovery; the recovery was not removed by the non-GAAP reconciliation. [S05]

Reported: For the quarter ended September 30, 2026, GAAP operating cash flow was USD 2,868 million, including approximately USD 300 million of IEEPA recovery; capital expenditures were USD 749 million and non-GAAP free cash flow was USD 2,119 million. The comparable 2025 values were USD 4,639 million, USD 614 million and USD 4,025 million, respectively. Nine-month free cash flow was USD 6,306 million versus USD 4,745 million. Free cash flow equals operating cash flow less capital expenditures. [S05]

Reported: Pratt & Whitney reported operating profit of USD 608 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 versus USD 751 million a year earlier. Its non-GAAP adjusted operating profit was USD 968 million: a USD 363 million customer-bankruptcy charge was excluded, while approximately USD 160 million of IEEPA recovery remained in adjusted profit. [S05]

Reported: For April 1–June 30, 2026, GAAP sales were USD 24,708 million, net income attributable to common shareowners USD 2,139 million and diluted EPS USD 1.57. [S02] [S06]

Reported: Adjusted diluted EPS was USD 1.89 for that quarter. This company-defined non-GAAP measure excludes acquisition accounting and specified restructuring and other items. [S03] [S06]


## Balance sheet and capital allocation

Reported: At September 30, 2026, GAAP cash and equivalents were USD 9,173 million. Long-term debt was USD 31,490 million excluding current maturities of USD 5,679 million; separate short-term borrowings were USD 210 million. Operating lease liabilities are not included in these debt figures. [S05]

Reported: At June 30, 2026, GAAP cash and equivalents were USD 8,305 million. GAAP long-term debt was USD 31,858 million, excluding current maturities of USD 5,296 million and separate short-term borrowings of USD 229 million. [S02] [S06]


## Management and governance

Reported: The July results identify Chris Calio as chairman and CEO. The September succession filing names Jill Albertelli as Pratt & Whitney’s future president. [S03] [S04]


## Valuation context

Interpretation: value recurring segment profit and cash generation after separating cost recoveries, customer-credit charges and acquisition adjustments. The larger backlog does not establish delivery timing or profit. No current stock price, valuation multiple or price target is supplied. [S05]


## Catalysts

Reported: Announced September 15, 2026: Jill Albertelli is scheduled to succeed Shane Eddy as Pratt & Whitney president on January 1, 2027. Execution effects remain uncertain. [S04]


## Risks and thesis breakers

Interpretation: Powder-metal issues require accelerated GTF inspections and additional shop visits. Costs depend on inspection findings, overhaul capacity and customer compensation; deteriorating estimates would weaken a cash-recovery thesis. [S02]

Interpretation: raised cash guidance and a larger defense backlog strengthen the execution case, but the quarterly cash decline, recovery-assisted earnings and customer-credit charge limit confidence in recurring profit. A miss of the new cash-flow floor, repeat customer-credit losses or sustained defense sales contraction would weaken this thesis. These are analyst monitoring conditions, not company forecasts. [S05]


## Scenarios and open questions

Interpretation: upside combines aftermarket growth with profitable defense deliveries; downside includes engine remediation, supply constraints or contract overruns. No scenario probabilities or target prices are assigned. [S02]

Interpretation: Review scope: the current earnings comparison uses the complete issuer results releases. Changes in detailed engine-remediation cash obligations, customer-credit exposure and litigation remain unverified because the new SEC quarterly filing and current report, call transcript and presentation were not reviewed. Earlier filing-based risks retain their original evidence dates. [S05] [S06]


## Relationships and themes

Reported: Pratt & Whitney’s F135 powers Lockheed Martin’s F-35. This is a documented propulsion-platform relationship; shared defense exposure alone does not establish another customer relationship. [S01]

These ranges are forecasts. [S06] [S05]
