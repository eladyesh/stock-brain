# AVAV — AeroVironment, Inc.

[Sector](../../README.md) · [Master index](../../../../INDEX.md)

> Evidence snapshot through 2026-10-18. Research status is recorded in metadata; this is not a live market feed.

## Business overview

Reported: AeroVironment develops autonomous, precision-strike, counter-drone, space and cyber capabilities for defense customers. The latest quarter covered here ended August 1, 2026. [S02]


## Revenue model and segments

Reported: The reportable segments are Autonomous Systems (AxS) and Space, Cyber and Directed Energy (SCDE). Revenue comes from hardware, support, customer-funded development and mission services, principally through fixed-price and cost-plus contracts. [S01]


## Products and services

Reported: Examples include Puma and JUMP reconnaissance aircraft, Switchblade loitering munitions, Titan counter-drone systems and LOCUST laser systems. Product families address different missions; their technical capabilities should not be treated as interchangeable. [S01]


## Customers and end markets

Reported: Disclosed buyers include U.S. military services, intelligence agencies, allied governments and government subcontractors. U.S. Army purchases can include orders for other defense organizations; country exposure must therefore distinguish end users from contracting agencies. [S01]


## Industry and value chain

Reported: The company combines engineering, supplier management, assembly, integration, testing and field support. Contract awards, appropriations, component availability and customer acceptance affect delivery timing. [S01]


## Competitive landscape

Reported: The annual report identifies Lockheed Martin and RTX among loitering-munitions and counter-drone competitors, and Anduril and Elbit among precision-strike competitors. These are company-identified competitive overlaps, not verified market-share rankings. [S01]


## Moat and differentiation

Interpretation: integrated autonomy, installed products and field support may support repeat procurement. This does not establish a durable moat: government intellectual-property rights and rapid technology changes constrain exclusivity. [S01]


## Growth drivers

Management guidance: Management prioritizes manufacturing capacity and supply-chain expansion to meet autonomous-system demand. Conversion of funded orders into deliveries is the test; management commentary is not an independent demand forecast. [S03]

Reported: The latest quarter also showed Switchblade order delays and lower SCDE revenue associated with the terminated SCAR program and reduced cyber-service contract scope. Consolidated expansion should not be generalized to every product line. [S02]


## Financial performance

Reported: For May 1–August 1, 2026, GAAP revenue was USD 480.490 million, versus USD 454.676 million in the prior fiscal first quarter ended August 2, 2025. GAAP net loss was USD 5.066 million; GAAP diluted loss per share was USD 0.10. [S03]

Reported: For that same fiscal 2027 first quarter, company-defined non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA was USD 53.4 million and adjusted diluted EPS was USD 0.59. These exclude specified expenses and are not GAAP profit or operating cash flow. [S03]

Derived: Calculated revenue growth for those fiscal first quarters was 5.68%. Acquisition contributions and differing product trends mean this consolidated rate is not a pure organic-growth measure. [S03] [S02]


## Balance sheet and capital allocation

Reported: At August 1, 2026, GAAP cash and equivalents were USD 278.390 million, short-term investments USD 301.837 million and long-term debt carrying value USD 730.057 million. [S02]

Reported: GAAP operating cash inflow was USD 13.496 million for May 1–August 1, 2026. Positive operating cash flow should be assessed alongside capital spending and increases in inventory and unbilled receivables. [S02]


## Management and governance

Reported: The August 14 proxy identifies Wahid Nawabi as chair, president and CEO, and Sean Woodward as CFO; September disclosures confirm those roles. September 24 shareholders elected the nominated directors and approved executive compensation on an advisory basis. [S05] [S03] [S04]


## Valuation context

Interpretation: compare GAAP earnings, cash generation and reconciled adjusted EBITDA separately because acquisition amortization materially affects reported profit. No current price, enterprise value or target multiple is supplied; market valuation remains unresearched. [S03]


## Catalysts

Interpretation: Monitoring trigger: the next fiscal 2027 results should test backlog conversion, production expansion and the reiterated annual outlook. The fiscal year ends April 30, 2027; no date for the next earnings release was verified. [S03] [S02]


## Risks and thesis breakers

Reported: The SCAR agreement was terminated for convenience in March 2026. The latest quarterly report also discloses continuing weaknesses in BlueHalo IT controls and goodwill-review controls, plus related securities litigation; allegations are not findings of liability. [S02] [S01]


## Scenarios and open questions

Interpretation: upside requires profitable deliveries; downside includes order delays or impairments. Verify control remediation, organic growth and cash conversion before strengthening a thesis. [S02]


## Relationships and themes

Reported: BlueHalo was acquired on May 1, 2025; ESAero on March 16, 2026. These are ownership relationships. Shared autonomy and counter-drone themes alone establish no commercial relationship. [S02]


## Recent developments
