# LMT — Lockheed Martin Corporation

[Sector](../../README.md) · [Master index](../../../../INDEX.md)

> Evidence snapshot through 2026-10-18. Research status is recorded in metadata; this is not a live market feed.

## Business overview

Reported: Lockheed Martin designs, builds and sustains aerospace, missile, naval and space systems, principally for U.S. government agencies and allied governments. [S01]


## Revenue model and segments

Reported: The four reporting segments are Aeronautics, Missiles and Fire Control, Rotary and Mission Systems, and Space. Sales include products and services. [S02]


## Products and services

Reported: Major programs include F-35 aircraft, PAC-3 and THAAD interceptors, HIMARS launchers, Sikorsky helicopters, Aegis combat systems and Orion spacecraft. [S01]


## Customers and end markets

Reported: U.S. military agencies and NASA are disclosed customers. International sales use both direct contracts and foreign military sales through the U.S. government. [S01]


## Industry and value chain

Reported: The prime contractor depends on specialized suppliers, scarce materials and government funding. Supplier disruption can raise costs and delay program delivery. [S01]


## Competitive landscape

Reported: Company-identified peers include Boeing, General Dynamics, Northrop Grumman, L3Harris and RTX. A peer may compete on one program and partner on another. [S01]


## Moat and differentiation

Interpretation: program incumbency, technical integration and sustainment capabilities support repeat work; procurement competition and government intellectual-property rights limit exclusivity. [S01]


## Growth drivers

Reported: The July results attributed missile growth to PAC-3, THAAD and Precision Strike Missile production increases. Continued growth requires successful manufacturing expansion. [S03]


## Financial performance

Reported: For March 30–June 28, 2026, GAAP sales were USD 20,063 million, net earnings USD 1,836 million and diluted EPS USD 7.94. [S02]

Reported: Year-over-year profit comparisons benefit from the absence of large prior-year program losses. The rebound should not be treated entirely as recurring operational improvement. [S03]


## Balance sheet and capital allocation

Reported: GAAP cash and equivalents were USD 3,791 million at June 28, 2026. GAAP operating cash inflow was USD 3,455 million for January 1–June 28, 2026. [S02]


## Management and governance

Reported: The July 23, 2026 results identify Jim Taiclet as chairman, president and CEO. Detailed compensation and ownership analysis remains outside this pilot. [S03]


## Valuation context

Interpretation: evaluate cash flow and earnings after distinguishing program charges and pension adjustments. No verified current share price, multiple or fair-value estimate is included. [S03]


## Catalysts

Interpretation: Monitoring trigger: subsequent results should test the higher July 2026 outlook and missile-production ramps. The next earnings announcement date was not sourced. [S03]


## Risks and thesis breakers

Interpretation: Concentrated exposure to F-35, complex fixed-price development and supplier performance can produce delays or losses. Repeat material program charges would weaken an execution-improvement thesis. [S01]


## Scenarios and open questions

Interpretation: upside requires profitable backlog conversion; downside includes program overruns or delayed funding. Test margin quality and working-capital needs before extrapolating the quarter. [S03]


## Relationships and themes

Reported: RTX is both a named competitor and Lockheed Martin’s Javelin joint-venture partner. The Javelin relationship is specific; it does not imply cooperation across all programs. [S01]


## Recent developments

Reported: On August 24, 2026, Lockheed Martin entered a replacement revolving credit agreement and extended another facility. The related filing reports no borrowing at the new facility’s closing. [S04]
