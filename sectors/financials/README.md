# Financials

[Company index](INDEX.md) · [Master index](../../INDEX.md)

## Sector overview

Banking, insurance, capital markets, and other financial services.

This page is a research guide. It does not contain a completed sector investment assessment.

## Industry map

The map below uses examples from the SEC industry-code list to organize research. It is a project grouping, not an official GICS constituent classification. A reported SIC code describes a business category; it does not establish every activity of a diversified issuer.

| Business family | Reported SIC examples | What to establish from company evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deposit-taking institutions | 6021, 6022, 6029, 6035, 6036 | Assess funding composition, interest-rate exposure, loan concentrations and capital ratios using issuer-specific definitions. |
| Credit and financing | 6111, 6141, 6153, 6159, 6162, 6163, 6172 | Identify who funds the loans, who retains credit risk, and whether revenue comes from interest, fees or securitization. |
| Capital-market services | 6200, 6211, 6221, 6282 | Separate trading, exchange, brokerage and asset-management economics; distinguish assets managed from company assets. |
| Insurance | 6311, 6321, 6324, 6331, 6351, 6361, 6399, 6411 | Distinguish risk underwriting from brokerage. Use line-specific loss, reserve, investment and capital evidence. |

The [company index](INDEX.md) lists actual factual files, their classification basis and financial-data availability. Ambiguous and fund-related records remain in the [unclassified directory](../unclassified/INDEX.md). Individual products, customers, competitive positions and investment conclusions still require company-specific sources.

## Research questions

- Which interest-rate, credit, underwriting, or market exposures matter?
- What capital and liquidity constraints limit growth or distributions?

## Metrics to investigate

- Return on equity
- Net interest margin or underwriting result
- Credit losses
- Capital and liquidity ratios

Select metrics that fit each business model. These are research prompts, not reported figures or standardized definitions across all companies.
