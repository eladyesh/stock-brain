# MSFT — Microsoft Corporation

[Sector](../../README.md) · [Master index](../../../../INDEX.md)

> Evidence snapshot through 2026-10-18. Research status is recorded in metadata; this is not a live market feed.

## Business overview

Reported: Microsoft sells business and consumer software, cloud infrastructure, devices, advertising, and gaming. Customers pay for subscriptions, usage, licenses, hardware, and services. Its Nasdaq common-stock ticker is MSFT. [S01]


## Revenue model and segments

Reported: FY2026 used Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing. Effective FY2027, reporting changes to Agents and Infra and Devices and Consumer. Azure becomes more narrowly consumption-based infrastructure; historical segment comparisons require the September recast. [S04]


## Products and services

Reported: The September reporting presentation groups Microsoft 365 and GitHub with Azure in Agents and Infra. This combines enterprise applications and agents with the infrastructure that runs workloads; Devices and Consumer includes Windows, Xbox, and search/advertising. [S04]


## Customers and end markets

Reported: Distribution combines direct enterprise and public-sector sales, partners serving organizations of varied sizes, OEM software preinstallation, and digital marketplaces. Enterprise consumption, employee subscriptions, PC purchasing, advertising, and gaming create distinct demand exposures. [S01]


## Industry and value chain

Interpretation: Microsoft operates cloud infrastructure and sells the applications above it. In July management described its own Maia and Cobalt chips alongside NVIDIA and AMD hardware; datacenter deployment and utilization constrain how quickly demand becomes billable capacity. [S03]


## Competitive landscape

Reported: Competition differs by product: Office faces established and AI-first application vendors; Dynamics faces cloud and on-premises business software; Azure faces competing infrastructure and platform services. This packet does not establish relative market shares. [S01]


## Moat and differentiation

Interpretation: integrating enterprise applications, identity, developer tools, and infrastructure may reduce customer integration work and increase switching costs. Product breadth and distribution support that mechanism, but do not establish permanent pricing power or immunity from substitutes. [S04] [S01]


## Growth drivers

Interpretation: Copilot adoption, premium Microsoft 365 products, and Azure consumption can expand spending within existing accounts. The July call attributed Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud growth to premium products and seat additions; sustained growth requires useful deployment and sufficient capacity, not merely signed contracts. [S03]


## Financial performance

Reported: For FY2026 ended June 30, GAAP revenue was USD 331,839 million, operating income USD 155,237 million, net income USD 133,749 million, and diluted EPS USD 17.95. FY2025 revenue was USD 281,724 million. Net income includes investment effects; the issuer separately reconciles non-GAAP results excluding OpenAI investment impacts. [S02]


## Balance sheet and capital allocation

Reported: At June 30, 2026, cash, equivalents, and short-term investments were USD 76,843 million. FY2026 operating cash flow was USD 182,935 million and cash additions to property and equipment were USD 115,948 million. Cash capex excludes noncash finance-lease additions; lease obligations also matter when assessing financial capacity. [S02] [S03]


## Management and governance

Interpretation: The September 15 announcement identifies Satya Nadella as chairman and CEO, Amy Hood as CFO, and Sandra E. Peterson as lead independent director. Combining chair and CEO roles makes independent board oversight relevant; a full compensation and ownership review remains outstanding. [S05]


## Valuation context

Not available: A current share price, fully reconciled enterprise value, and forward consensus were not sourced. No current multiple or fair-value estimate is asserted.

## Catalysts

Interpretation: The issuer lists October 28, 2026 for FY2027 first-quarter earnings. Interpretation: the release should test cloud demand and spending efficiency and provide the first quarterly reporting under the new segment structure. A scheduled release does not imply a favorable result. [S06] [S08]


## Risks and thesis breakers

Interpretation: Material risks include cybersecurity failures, competition, regulatory constraints, and cloud capacity or supply shortfalls. Interpretation: persistent weak utilization or cloud monetization relative to infrastructure spending would undermine the growth thesis even if reported revenue keeps rising. [S01]


## Scenarios and open questions

Interpretation: Scenario assumptions: bull—AI applications and Azure utilization reinforce each other; base—growth continues with capital demands; bear—capacity commitments outlast profitable demand. Track cash conversion, lease obligations, and comparable segment margins. These are research hypotheses without assigned probabilities or price targets. [S03]


## Relationships and themes

Reported: Microsoft and Palantir announced a government-cloud and classified-network partnership on August 8, 2024. It combines Azure infrastructure and Azure OpenAI with Palantir platforms; the announcement made classified availability subject to authorization. This is an integration relationship, not evidence of exclusivity or identical competition. [S07]


## Recent developments

Reported: September 2, 2026: Microsoft announced its FY2027 segment redesign. September 15: the board declared a USD 0.98 quarterly dividend per share, payable December 10 to holders of record November 19. The declaration is not a completed payment. [S05] [S08]
